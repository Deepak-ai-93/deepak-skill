# EEAT + Anti-Fluff Reference — blog-seo-content

Same playbook as the repo's email-marketing skill, adapted for long-form articles.

---

## EEAT — the four pillars in a blog article

| Pillar | In an article this looks like | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Experience** | Firsthand story, screenshots, "we ran this", real numbers from your own work | "We cut our own signup time from 40 to 6 minutes — here's the exact change that did it." |
| **Expertise** | Named author + credential + bio link, accurate specifics, no overclaiming | "By Priya Nair, Head of Product — led the redesign referenced throughout." |
| **Authoritativeness** | Every stat has a named source with a link; mentions of real tools/studies/press | "Per Gartner's 2025 research on email compliance, 83% of deliverability failures trace to authentication." |
| **Trustworthiness** | Honest limitations, transparent intent, updated dates, easy to verify | "These results are from our own 4,000-user cohort — your mileage may vary, and here's why." |

**Rules:**
- Never "studies show" — name the study, link it.
- Never "best-in-class" without a comparison or receipt.
- The author bio is a required block at the top or bottom: name, role, credential, link.
- Update the "last updated" date honestly.

## The anti-fluff blocklist (same as email-marketing — never write these)
`unlock` · `game-changer` · `elevate` · `supercharge` · `level up` · `unleash` · `boost` · `empower` · `revolutionize` · `optimize` (as filler) · `leverage` · `journey` · `transform your` · `in today's fast-paced world` · `it's not just X, it's Y` · `skyrocket` · `crush it` · `secrets to` (unless literal) · `click here` (as anchor text).

## GEO / AI-citation blocks (make it quotable)
1. **Answer in the first 100 words** — a direct, complete answer to the H1 question (AI engines lift this for featured answers).
2. **"Bottom line" block** — 3–5 bullet takeaways at the end.
3. **Tables and lists** for data — structured facts get quoted more than prose.
4. **FAQ-style H3s** for People-Also-Ask questions.
5. **Concise definitions** — "X is…" in one or two sentences.

## The 8 article copy rules
1. Answer the question immediately (first 100 words).
2. One idea per H2 — if an H2 has two ideas, split it.
3. Specific beats generic — numbers, names, receipts everywhere.
4. Verbs over adjectives.
5. Write like a person talks — no corporate voice.
6. Every claim has proof or is flagged as opinion.
7. Internal links use descriptive anchors ("see our pricing teardown"), never "click here".
8. End with a specific next step (not "thanks for reading!").
