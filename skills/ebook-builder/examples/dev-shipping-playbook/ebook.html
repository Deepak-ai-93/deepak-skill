The Shipping Playbook

Five steps that took our deploy time from 4 days to 40 minutes.

SCENE: a developer's dual monitors at 2am, terminal full of green checkmarks, one merge button highlighted
step 01

Ship the smallest thing that works.

Big PRs rot. A 4,000-line diff sits for weeks, then dies in review. The 40-minute deploy started with one rule: every merge ships something usable.

avg PR size: 4,000 → 350 lines
SCENE: a git log screen, each commit one green dot, a reviewer clicking "approve" in seconds
step 02

Automate the boring gate.

Lint, tests, and type checks ran on a dev's machine — where everyone "forgot." Now they run in CI before review ever starts. Humans review logic; machines review style.

"If a bot can check it, a human shouldn't."

SCENE: a CI pipeline dashboard, every check a green square, a red one auto-blocking a merge with a comment
step 03

Review in hours, not days.

A review SLA turned waiting from a black hole into a contract. Small diffs + a 4-hour SLA meant merges stopped stacking. Throughput doubled with zero new hires.

time-to-merge: 96h → 3.2h
SCENE: two developers over a laptop at a whiteboard, a clock showing the 4-hour review SLA, a merge button clicked
step 04

Deploy like it's nothing.

Feature flags made the deploy boring — so we shipped daily. When a release stopped being an event, it stopped being a risk. Rollbacks became one command.

"You should be slightly embarrassed by your deploy process — if it's impressive, it's too rare."

SCENE: a one-click deploy button on a dashboard, a green banner reading "deployed to production", a developer shrugging
step 05

Measure the pipeline, not the heroics.

Lead time, deploy frequency, change-failure rate — tracked on one board. The heroics stopped because the metrics made the system visible. 4 days → 40 minutes in 90 days.

lead time: 4 days → 40 min · deploys: 2/mo → 22/mo
SCENE: a wall board with four DORA metrics trending down to green, a team standing around it
