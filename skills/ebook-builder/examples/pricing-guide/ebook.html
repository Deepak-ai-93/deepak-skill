The 5-Page Pricing Fix

The framework that tripled our revenue per visitor in 30 days.

SCENE: a founder's laptop at 11pm, one warm lamp, a pricing page with a single highlighted button
Mistake 01

You sell features, not outcomes.

Nobody buys software — they buy the 3 hours saved every week. Your pricing page lists capabilities; the winners list the result.

3 hours saved / week > 40 features
SCENE: a pricing page split in two — a features table on one side, a single outcome sentence on the other
Mistake 02

The price hides behind a demo.

If a buyer has to talk to you to learn the price, they're gone. The demo call became a filter — and it filtered out the buyers who didn't need hand-holding.

"If I have to call you to see a price, I'm out." — a founder we lost

SCENE: a closed laptop at a desk, a sticky note reading "book a demo" crossed out, a price tag drawn over it
Mistake 03

One plan leaves buyers guessing.

With no anchor, nobody can judge "is this fair?" — so nobody judged at all. A reference plan makes the mid-tier look reasonable and the choice obvious.

3 tiers > 1 tier > 0 tiers
SCENE: three pricing cards on a screen, the middle card highlighted with a "most popular" tag
Mistake 04

You watch signups, not the funnel.

We measured the result and ignored the page that produced it. $12,400 went into traffic while the pricing page converted at 0.4%.

Instrument the page, not the outcome.

SCENE: a dashboard showing a flat signup line while a smaller chart below shows a 0.4% pricing-page conversion
The Fix

One page. One price. One outcome.

The swap took one afternoon: "Book a demo" became "$29/month — cancel anytime." Revenue per visitor tripled in 30 days, and support time dropped — the price did the qualifying for us.

3× revenue per visitor · −40% support time
SCENE: the same laptop, now showing a clean pricing page with one highlighted price — the lamp still on, the founder smiling
