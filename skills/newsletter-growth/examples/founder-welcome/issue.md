# Pricing with receipts — issue draft

**Subject:** The button that cost us $12,400 — *(curiosity gap · 39/60 chars)*
**Audience:** SaaS founders · **Voice:** first-person, founder · **Goal:** replies + forwards

## The open (hook)

It was 11pm on a Tuesday when I found the button. Four months of flat signups, and the answer was one line of copy we'd written on day one — "Book a demo" — sitting above a field we'd never tested. I sat there for a minute, then opened the analytics to confirm what I already suspected: nobody had ever clicked it. Not once. Not ever.

## The stakes (rising tension)

I'd blamed everything else first: the ads, the landing page, the market. $12,400 went into paid traffic while the pricing page quietly converted at 0.4%. The worst part wasn't the money — it was that our own dashboard never showed the page that caused it. We were watching signups, not the funnel that produced them. Every week the board asked why growth was flat, and every week we pointed at the traffic numbers instead of the page that was killing them. The button was the difference between a growth conversation and a funeral.

## The payoff

The fix took one afternoon: one page, one price, one outcome. "Book a demo" became "$29/month — cancel anytime." Revenue per visitor tripled in 30 days, and support time dropped because the price did the qualifying for us. The button wasn't broken. The promise was. We stopped selling a meeting and started selling an outcome with a number on it — and buyers made the decision themselves, at 11pm, without us.

## Value — the receipts

- The exact swap: "Book a demo" → "$29/month, cancel anytime" — one line, one afternoon, no redesign
- Revenue per visitor: 3× in 30 days (same traffic, same offer, different promise)
- Support time: −40% — the price filtered out the tire-kickers before they booked a call
- The metric that caught it: pricing-page conversion, not signups — instrument the page, not the result
- The rule we now live by: if a page has a field, test it. Every field is a question you're asking the customer.
