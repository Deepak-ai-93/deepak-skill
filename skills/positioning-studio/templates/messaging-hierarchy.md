# Messaging hierarchy — the four rungs (reference for Stage 2–3)

Every piece of content lifts the SAME four rungs. Fill them once in `positioning.md`, then rephrase per channel in `message-map.md`.

## The rungs

| Rung | Question it answers | Rule | Weak → strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Audience** | Who is this for? | ONE specific person — a role + a situation, not a market segment | "SaaS companies" → "a 3-person agency owner who does the billing herself" |
| **Problem** | What hurts them before you? | Their words, not your feature list; name the cost of ignoring it | "Poor project management" → "client work piles up because nothing tracks revisions" |
| **Promise** | What do they get? | ONE outcome, measurable; the promise the one-liner makes | "Better collaboration" → "approve client revisions in one afternoon, not one week" |
| **Proof** | Why believe it? | A specific receipt — number, named customer, before/after | "Teams love us" → "Cut revision cycles 40% for 60+ design teams" |

## Building order (fast path)

1. **Proof first** — inventory what you can honestly claim. This decides how big the promise may be.
2. **Audience + Problem** — the pain you actually have receipts for.
3. **Promise** — the outcome that pain wants; sized to the proof.
4. **One-liner** = Audience + Promise + difference, ≤ 15 words.

## Per-channel rephrasing (the message map)

Same rungs, different phrasing — never a new message:

| Channel | What carries the message |
|---|---|
| Landing page | Hero = one-liner; value prop = promise + proof |
| Email | Subject = problem; first line = promise; proof in the body |
| Social / thread hook | Tagline (the one that fits the platform's voice) |
| Ebook cover | Promise phrased as the open loop |
| Ad | Hook = problem in the audience's words; CTA = promise |

## Anti-drift checklist

- [ ] The one-liner, the promise rung and every tagline agree
- [ ] Every claim in the message map traces back to a proof point
- [ ] No channel introduced a message the hierarchy doesn't contain
