# Positioning card — fill this at Stage 3 (the format IS the audit contract)

The final `positioning.md` must contain EXACTLY these sections in this order. The audit (`audit-positioning.mjs`) reads this structure — missing or renamed sections fail.

## The card

```
# Positioning — {product / brand}

## One-liner
{ONE sentence, ≤ 15 words: audience + outcome + difference. In the author's voice.}

## Messaging hierarchy
- Audience: {who, specifically — a person, not a market segment}
- Problem: {the pain they feel BEFORE the product — their words, not yours}
- Promise: {the outcome the product delivers — one outcome, measurable}
- Proof: {why it's believable — the strongest proof point, one line}

## Proof points
- {specific, honest bullet: number, named customer, before/after, metric}
- {…}
- {…}   (≥ 3 — no proof? shrink the promise until there is)

## Taglines
- {standalone hook, ≤ 8 words}
- {…}
- {…}   (≥ 3, each different — one for hooks, one for email, one for the cover)

## Voice guide
- Tone: {from the taste profile — e.g. direct + confident, warm + plain}
- Rhythm: {short declarative / long flowing / mixed}
- Pet phrases (use sparingly): {…}
- **Banned words** (the audit FAILs the positioning on these):
  - {word}
  - {word}
```

## Writing rules (the rails)

- **One-liner:** say it out loud. If it takes two breaths or a caveat ("…for teams who…"), cut it. The best one-liners are boring-true, not clever ("We help freelancers get paid in 3 days" beats "Revolutionizing the payments experience").
- **Proof over hype:** every adjective in the promise must have a proof point behind it. "Fast" needs a number. "Loved" needs a named customer.
- **The author's voice:** the one-liner and taglines especially — their rhythm, their words, zero buzzwords. If a line could have been written for any product, rewrite it.
- **Anti-drift:** the promise in the hierarchy is the SAME promise in the one-liner, the taglines and the message map. If they disagree, the positioning isn't finished.
