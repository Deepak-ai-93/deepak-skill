# Media kit — template for sponsorship-pipeline

The media kit is **the offer, not the resume**. A brand manager reads it in 30 seconds and sees: *this creator reaches the exact people my campaign needs.* One page of proof.

## The template (fill per creator)

```
# {Name} — media kit

## The niche + the audience
Niche: {niche — the ONE thing the audience buys}
Audience: {who they are — role, age, interests — 1–2 lines}

## Proof (engagement, not just followers)
| Platform | Followers | Avg views/impressions | Engagement | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| YouTube | 50k | 250k/mo | 6% avg CTR · 45% retention | tutorials |
| Newsletter | 12k | 68% open | 9% click | weekly |

## The offer
Formats:
- {Integrated video/post mention — the product woven into regular content}
- {Dedicated sponsored video/post}
- {Bundle — mention + dedicated + newsletter/IG story}
Placements: {where the sponsor shows up — intro, mid-roll, end-card, newsletter slot}
Deliverables: {what the sponsor receives — draft for approval, analytics report after, clips}
Timeline: {typical turnaround + report date}

## A past result (proof the offer works)
{One real example: the campaign, the audience reached, the engagement — numbers only, no guarantees}

## Contact
{email / DM / booking link}
```

## The rules

- **The sponsor's problem leads.** The first line names who the audience is and why a brand in {niche} needs them. The creator's name is the header, not the pitch.
- **Engagement beats followers.** Views, saves, replies, retention, open rates — the numbers that predict campaign performance. If you only have followers, say so honestly and show what you do have.
- **One past result.** One real, dated example with numbers. Never fabricate — a fake result is a career-ending leak risk.
- **No guarantees.** The kit shows what the audience DID, never what a campaign WILL do.
- **FTC-ready.** The kit notes that every sponsored post ships with #ad / #sponsored (disclosure is a feature, not a fine).
