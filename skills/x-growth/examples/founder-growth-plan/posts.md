### Post 2 · value · Pricing lessons  *(659/800 chars)*

The fastest pricing fix I know takes ten minutes.

Your pricing page does one job: give a stranger enough certainty to pay. Most pages fail it because they argue. Try this instead.

- Price the outcome, not the product. "Invoices that pay themselves" beats "online invoicing software" every single time.
- Show one plan before three. An anchor makes the middle plan look reasonable; no anchor makes all of them look like guesses.
- Put the price where the eye lands first. If a visitor has to scroll to find it, they have already left.

Do those three and your page stops explaining and starts selling.

Save this — it is the ten minutes that pays for itself.

### Post 4 · proof · Positioning & messaging  *(583/800 chars)*

Three headlines, 30 days, 41% more signups. Same product.

We tested three hero lines on identical traffic and the winner was the one that said less. Positioning is subtraction.

- Line A described the product: "Online invoicing for freelancers." Flat.
- Line B named the outcome: "Invoices that pay themselves." 41% more signups than A.
- Line C tried to be clever. It lost to B by 18 points — the feed has no room for clever.

Words moved revenue more than any feature launch we shipped that quarter.

Steal the method: write the outcome, delete the adjectives, test it for a week.

### Post 5 · value · Positioning & messaging  *(503/800 chars)*

If your pricing page needs a paragraph to explain itself, it is not priced yet.

I wrote this rule after reading fifty founder pricing pages. The ones that sold had the same shape.

- One line of what it does — the outcome, not the features.
- One line of who it is for — the exact person, not "anyone who works".
- One line of what it costs — a number, not "contact us".

The best pages read like a text message: short, specific, done.
